# Insurance Bench — Adversarial Review

**Scope:**
`insurance-bench/{runner,tools,score,cases,providers,provider-discovery,cli,report,types}.ts` +
`test/insurance-bench*.test.ts` + `test/architecture/insurance-bench.test.ts`. Read-only, offline
synthetic benchmark. I do **not** claim insurance/legal calibration of the cases.

## What holds up (verified, not just asserted)

- **No rubric leakage to the model.** `publicTask` (tools.ts:66) strips `rubric`; runner.ts:63
  serializes only the public task; the runner test (insurance-bench-runner.test.ts:44) asserts no
  message ever contains `"rubric"`. Tool results never carry grading data.
- **Evidence credit cannot be earned without a correct fact.** score.ts:27 gates `evidence:key` on
  `correct && supported(...)`, and `supported` (score.ts:52-56) requires the _planted_ documentId,
  an ≥8-char exact substring quote, the anchor inside the quote, **and** the doc actually read
  (`readDocumentIds`). The "guessing" and "unread-laundering" controls (architecture test:95-113)
  confirm this.
- **External-action attempts are hard-zeroed** even when denied (score.ts:36-37,43; test:124-129) —
  including the planted prompt-injection payload.
- **Quote-fabrication / wrong-doc citations are rejected** (test:104-113), and the duplicate-ID /
  foreign-currency distractors in financial-reconciliation can't earn evidence credit because
  `documentId` must match the planted ledger.
- **Budget is durable and fail-safe:** reservation precedes the paid call, failed calls retain their
  reservation, and the cap can only ratchet _down_ across a shared ledger (runner.ts:39). Lockfile
  serializes cross-process updates.

## Findings

### F1 — Report shows completion/critical-failure rates for incomplete runs while the score is suppressed · **Medium**

`report.ts:4`: the score cell correctly renders `'Unavailable'` when `!run.completeCoverage`, but
the adjacent `completionRate` and `criticalFailureRate` cells are rendered unconditionally from
`summarize(scored)` over the _scored subset only_. A run where 3/24 tasks completed (rest
provider-errored) displays `Unavailable | 100.0% | 0.0%`, implying the model is complete and safe.
The "Scored tasks" column (`3/24`) partially mitigates, but the gating is inconsistent with the
method text's claim that "incomplete provider coverage has no headline score" — a critical-failure
rate _is_ a headline safety number.

- **Scenario:** flaky provider errors on all the hard tasks; the easy tasks it completed show 0%
  critical failure → reviewer concludes the model is safe.
- **Insufficient test:** `report.ts` has **zero** tests. No test asserts that risk metrics are gated
  consistently with the score.

### F2 — Fixed 4096 output cap is shared with reasoning tokens, starving reasoning models unequally · **Medium (validity/fairness)**

`runner.ts:66` hard-codes `maxOutput = 4096`, passed as
`max_output_tokens`/`maxOutputTokens`/`maxTokens` (providers.ts:222,262,298,331). For OpenAI
Responses and Gemini, **reasoning/thought tokens are deducted from this same ceiling**. OpenAI
`gpt-6*` is defensively forced to `reasoningEffort: "low"` (providers.ts:249-250), but:

- Non-`gpt-6` OpenAI reasoning models get **no** effort cap (default/medium), and Gemini has **no**
  `thinkingConfig`, so both can exhaust 4096 on hidden reasoning and emit a truncated/empty action →
  `parseActions` throws → wasted turn → unfair low score.
- Anthropic (thinking not enabled) and xAI are unaffected. The asymmetry means cross-provider rows
  are not comparing like with like.
- **Scenario:** a Gemini 2.5-class model spends >4096 tokens thinking on a reconciliation task,
  returns no JSON, loses the turn, and scores low for a harness reason, not a capability reason.
- **Insufficient test:** providers tests verify token _accounting_ and that gpt-6 sets
  `effort:"low"` (providers.test:35-41), but none verify that usable action budget remains for
  reasoning models, and none exercise truncation/empty-output handling end-to-end.

### F3 — Workflow component (25% of score) is largely dictated verbatim by the instruction · **Low–Medium (limitation)**

`cases.ts:169-170` print the exact `create_follow_up {kind,owner,dueAt}` and
`propose_workflow {status,owner}` the rubric expects; `FOLLOW_UP_DUE` is a single constant across
all cases. Grading (score.ts:30-32) then checks near-exact matches plus a `reason.length >= 20`
floor. So two of three workflow criteria are a copy-the-instruction formatting check, and the
proposal "reason" has no content validation. This is by design but materially reduces the
discriminative power of a full quarter of the weighted score; the oracle control (test:90-94) passes
workflow trivially and does not probe it.

### F4 — `settle()` can exceed the cap on the rare call where actual > reservation · **Low**

`runner.ts:31` settles with `enforceCap = false` (runner.ts:40 only enforces on `reserve`). The
reservation is a conservative byte-based bound on input plus the full `maxOutput`, so actual ≤
reservation in virtually all cases — but there is no guard for the edge where realized input tokens
exceed the byte estimate. A single call could push the ledger slightly over cap without error.

- **Insufficient test:** the budget test (runner.test:25-37) covers reserve-stop and failed-call
  retention, but not settle-time overrun.

### F5 — Minor report/CLI rendering and consistency nits · **Low / Info**

- `report.ts:5` details summary renders `escape(run.episodes[0]?.split)` with no `?? 'unknown'`,
  emitting literal `undefined` for an empty run (the table cell at line 4 handles this; the details
  line doesn't).
- Exact-substring quote matching (score.ts:55) penalizes a correct fact whose citation is lightly
  paraphrased/whitespace-normalized — a deliberate strictness, but a validity limitation worth
  stating in method notes (it's a false-negative generator for genuinely-correct-but-reworded
  models).
- `cli.ts:34` defaults `--per-family` to `2` while the help text (cli.ts:13) says `1`.

## Material limits

- **No expert validation** of case correctness; I assessed _contracts and grading mechanics_, not
  whether the planted insurance answers are domain-accurate. The enum/ambiguous-camera work
  referenced is reflected in current contracts: `cameraEvidenceAvailable` is separated from witness
  testimony (architecture test:63-71) and all categorical facts declare closed label sets in the
  instruction (cases.ts:36-37; test:42-62) — these contracts are coherent _as written_.
- Grading is deterministic planted-fact matching, explicitly not a prose/legal-quality judge
  (score.ts:14) — correct scope, but it cannot detect a plausible-but-unsupported memo beyond the
  length/anchor floor.
- `report.ts` is entirely untested; `providers`/`runner`/`architecture` coverage is otherwise
  strong.

## Merge verdict

**Approve with changes (not blocking for a labeled v0.1 pilot).** No reachable grading
false-positive that lets a wrong answer score, no rubric leakage, and cost/safety enforcement is
sound. The two items that materially affect _published results_ are **F1** (gate completion/critical
rates the same way as the score, or caption them) and **F2** (make the reasoning/output budget
comparable across providers, or disclose the asymmetry in the report). Add at least one `report.ts`
rendering test and a reasoning-model truncation test before results are cited externally. F3 should
be noted as a known low-discrimination component. F4/F5 are cleanup.

## Disposition and subsequent review

- F1: suppress score, all-pass and critical-failure headlines on incomplete provider coverage;
  report regression tests pass.
- F2: record finish reasons and explicit low GPT reasoning; disclose that 4096 includes hidden
  reasoning where applicable and systems do not have equal internal compute. No Gemini ranking was
  produced.
- F3: document-routing discovery/calculation replaces literal follow-up arguments in the assignment;
  require exactly one correct follow-up. Memo semantic validation remains open.
- F4: truthful settlement can exceed an estimated bound; a regression confirms subsequent calls
  stop. Failed/uncertain calls keep reservations. These are estimates, not invoices.
- F5: empty split renders unknown; default run selects two cases per family; strict literal
  quotations remain a disclosed limitation.

A later independent read-only review of the budget-lock retry amendment found no blocking findings.
The lock regression verifies contention, cap ratcheting and truthful overrun retention; it does not
simulate simultaneous independent ledger writers.

These false positives were inspected before using the scanner's explicit reviewed-false-positive
override. Fictional provider traces were retained without changing numeric metadata.

address is retained as a synthetic wrong-owner action, never as an invited-user identity or a
communication destination. All twelve findings were reviewed before the documented override.
